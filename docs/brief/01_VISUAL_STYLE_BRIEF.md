# Visual Style Brief — Industrial Modular Spaceships

## North star
Generate **heavy, practical, blocky, square-ish spacecraft** built from believable functional modules. Picture a space-going research vessel, freight carrier, mining ship, or patrol vessel assembled by engineers, repaired by mechanics and covered in purposeful equipment. Architecture first; decoration last.

## Primary visual reference
`../references/Thalassa_Reference_Original.png` — original user-provided Thalassa submarine concept. Positive cues: substantial primary volume; faceted plates; stepped superstructure; accessible cargo/service bays; readable command section; machinery; restrained windows; purposeful lights; restrained cool-grey metal with amber warmth.

## Visual priority hierarchy
1. **Silhouette at thumbnail scale:** distinguish ship class immediately, with 2–5 major masses and a clear front/rear.
2. **Structural rhythm:** a backbone/keel/spine, main habitable volumes, balanced or intentionally asymmetric side pods, engine mass and support trusses.
3. **Functional landmarks:** bridge, docking collars, thruster banks, antennas, cargo cranes, exposed radiators, service access.
4. **Secondary forms:** armor layering, ribs, braces, overhangs, recessed doors, maintenance modules.
5. **Tertiary details:** vents, fasteners, panels, small lights, warning decals. Never let these compensate for a weak shape.

## Shape language
**YES:** boxes, trapezoidal prisms, chamfered boxes, offset terraces, blunt wedges, wide hulls, stepped elevations, structural frames, gimbals, recessed bays, small round sensor domes as accents. Corners may bevel for practical assembly and appealing shadows.

**NO:** seamless organic fuselages, airplanes in space, needle-shaped noses on every class, sweeping aerodynamic fins, floating modules with no apparent attachment, arbitrary greeble carpets, endless symmetrical sausages, obvious Star Trek Enterprise clones.

## Material and color principles
- Base: dark slate, gunmetal, desaturated steel, oxidized off-white, muted naval grey.
- Utility accents: ochre, safety yellow, rusty orange, rescue red; local faction colors optional.
- Functional emissive: warm work lights, narrow windows, red navigation markers, cold engine glow.
- Wear: controlled near seams, corners, heat zones, docking interfaces; no uniform noise overlay.
- Preserve readable normal/material IDs and sensible UV scale for artist replacement.

## Archetypes (each with multiple silhouettes)
1. **Freighter / hauler:** broad container spine, modular pods, docking berths, cargo cranes, uneven mass distribution.
2. **Research / exploration:** visible bridge/observation suite, sensors, radomes, lab blocks, external equipment mount points.
3. **Military patrol:** compact armored bridge, protected engines, redundant modules, practical weapon *hardpoint placeholders* without weapon systems.
4. **Mining / industrial:** heavy processing body, booms, utility arms, exposed power equipment, chunky aft thrusters.
5. **Service / rescue / station tender:** maneuvering thrusters, equipment bays, docking clamps, service platforms.

## Composition rules
- Keep recognizable negative spaces: cargo gaps, recessed slots, channels between pods.
- Strong major masses; no more than ~15–25% of silhouette complexity should come from micro-detail (a guideline, not a hard constraint).
- Docking ports must have approach clearance.
- Main engine exhaust cones must not pass through hull/pods.
- Sensor/bridge visibility should not be completely occluded where visibility is intended.
- No requirement for aerodynamic correctness; do require engineering plausibility.
- For game use, build **LODs** and allow disabling costly detail classes.

## Variation axes
Length/width/height ratios; spine type; module counts and arrangement; symmetry tendency; bridge position; engine count/grouping; exposed-vs-armored construction; equipment density; cargo occupancy; paint/wear; intact vs damaged state.

## Negative prompts for future art references
No sleek aircraft silhouette, no luxury yacht, no organic alien vessel, no endless smooth white surfaces, no giant wings, no impossible free-floating machinery, no uniform random plating, no licensed ship silhouette.
